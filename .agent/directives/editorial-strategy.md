# Editorial Strategy and Composition

This directive governs how Jim's public content earns and uses attention. It covers audience,
reader purpose, information order, readability, section weight, evidence, platform fit and the
method used to review a draft.

Read it alongside [editorial-guidance.md](./editorial-guidance.md), which governs identity,
positioning, voice and register. Neither document replaces the other. Specific settled choices
belong in [Editorial Decision Records](../../docs/editorial/decision-records/).

## Authority and scope

| Surface                 | Owns                                                                                         |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| This directive          | Audience strategy, attention, structure, readability, length, evidence flow and platform fit |
| `editorial-guidance.md` | Identity, positioning, voice, register and recurring language constraints                    |
| EDRs                    | Specific editorial decisions and their alternatives                                          |
| Working drafts          | Current copy under discussion                                                                |
| Published content       | What readers can actually encounter now                                                      |

A draft is not strong merely because every sentence sounds like Jim. It must also help the right
reader notice, understand and trust the right things in the time and context they actually have.

## Begin with the reader's decision

Before editing, state:

1. the primary reader and any important secondary readers;
2. what each reader is trying to decide or do;
3. what they are likely to know, misunderstand or doubt at first contact;
4. the surface and reading mode: compact preview, scan, full read, print, search result or machine
   retrieval; and
5. what should be newly clear after reading.

Do not optimise for an abstract "professional audience" or for everyone at once. A secondary
audience may be served without controlling the order or vocabulary of the whole piece.

### Standing audiences

The CV and LinkedIn profile commonly serve:

1. **Hiring managers and senior leaders** deciding whether Jim's judgement fits a consequential
   problem.
2. **Recruiters** deciding whether they can recognise, place and forward the profile accurately.
3. **Peers and collaborators** deciding whether the work and way of thinking merit engagement.
4. **Founders, funders and public-interest leaders** looking for origination, leverage and
   responsibility without inflated commercial claims.
5. **Search and AI systems** retrieving structured facts and natural-language descriptions.

The primary human reader should not have to decode machine-facing keywords. Use structured data
and standard fields for retrieval where the surface supports them.

## Composition is surface-specific

The optimum composition for one surface is unlikely to be optimum for another. Shared identity
does not require shared order, length, paragraphing or repeated copy.

| Surface                    | Primary composition problem                                                                                                                           |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| LinkedIn                   | Win recognition in a fixed, fragmented interface; survive collapsed previews; let scanning readers move from card to About, current work and warrants |
| CV                         | Sustain a deliberate evidential read; use designed hierarchy and proportion; work in browser and print without platform-owned section order           |
| Front page                 | Create invitation and recognition through narrative, interests and values rather than compressing professional proof                                  |
| Search and social previews | Preserve identity and distinction in a short, context-poor projection                                                                                 |
| Structured data            | Support accurate retrieval and entity understanding rather than reproduce human narrative composition                                                 |

LinkedIn may need recognisable terms earlier, shorter independent modules and selective repetition
because readers enter through different sections and the platform truncates them. The CV can depend
more on page-level hierarchy, cumulative evidence and relationships between adjacent sections. The
front page can take a narrative route that would waste attention in either professional evidence
surface.

Review each surface on its own terms. Cross-surface consistency means the reader meets the same
person, facts and values—not that the artefacts use identical sentences or architecture.

## Design the attention path

Attention is finite and sequential. Put information in the order a reader needs it, not the order
in which it was discovered or argued about.

- **First contact supplies coordinates and distinction.** A reader should quickly know the kind of
  professional they are looking at and why this profile is not interchangeable with another.
- **Progressive disclosure earns depth.** The card earns the preview; the preview earns expansion;
  the overview earns inspection of current work; experience and linked artefacts warrant the
  claims.
- **Front-load meaning, not throat-clearing.** Qualification, provenance and context cannot rescue
  a vague opening that readers never expand.
- **Each layer adds a new function.** Repetition is useful only when the same idea does different
  work for a different reader or surface.
- **Test the real projections.** Review compact cards, collapsed previews, section headers, the
  first line of each role and the full profile. A document-only read is insufficient when the
  platform hides or truncates content.

## Structure and section weight

Length is an allocation of reader attention, not a target to fill.

- Give each section and paragraph one clear job. Split it when the reader must hold two different
  questions in mind.
- Give more space to recent, distinctive and decision-relevant evidence. Chronology does not imply
  equal weight.
- Delete a detail when its marginal value is lower than the interruption it creates.
- Do not let an organising theory recruit every role into proving it. Some entries should remain
  plain facts.
- Avoid a sequence of equally dense sections. Contrast helps the reader recognise what matters.
- Hard platform limits are constraints, not editorial goals. Verify current limits at the point of
  publication; do not fossilise changeable product numbers in this directive.

### Surface standards

#### Headline or profile card

- Has one job: provide recognisable coordinates and an earned reason to continue.
- Prefer two to four anchors and no more than one unfamiliar or interpretive phrase.
- Must remain intelligible when truncated; do not depend on the final phrase to explain the first.

#### About or positioning section

- The opening visible before expansion must establish identity, method or present contribution.
- Each paragraph should perform one movement and usually remain readable in a single screen-width
  block. A long paragraph must earn its weight through a coherent example or argument.
- Move from orientation to demonstrated method, then to present fit. Do not repeat the career
  thesis after the evidence has already made it visible.

#### Experience

- The current role may carry several distinct movements; label or separate them so they can be
  scanned independently.
- Each paragraph or bullet should make one contribution legible: action, decision, evidence or
  consequence.
- Earlier and shorter roles need enough detail to establish range or continuity, not a miniature
  positioning essay.
- Put attribution beside the claim it qualifies. Do not make readers reach a disclaimer before
  they can trust the sentence.

#### Skills, publications and featured work

- Skills support recognition and retrieval; they do not prove expertise.
- Publications and featured artefacts act as warrants. Their titles and first sentences must work
  without surrounding explanation.
- Curate rather than inventory. Include an item because it changes a reader's decision, not merely
  because it exists.

## Readability

Readability means preserving the real complexity while reducing unnecessary decoding work.

- Prefer concrete nouns and active verbs. Name the system, decision, artefact or consequence.
- Introduce an unfamiliar term before relying on it; expand acronyms when the target reader may not
  know them.
- Vary sentence length and rhythm. Do not stack several long, multi-clause sentences or several
  abstractions without a concrete anchor.
- Keep grammatical subjects close to their verbs and qualifications close to the claims they
  bound.
- Use headings and bullets when they expose real structure, not to disguise prose that has not been
  prioritised.
- Read important passages aloud. Breath, emphasis, hesitation and the urge to paraphrase are
  editorial evidence.
- Prefer plain language, but do not flatten precise or meaningful distinctions for a hypothetical
  reader who has no interest in the work.

## Evidence, trust and attribution

- Match the strength of the verb to the strength of the evidence.
- Participant records are evidence. Do not demand third-party proof for every true professional
  claim, and do not convert absence of independent proof into false uncertainty.
- Demonstrate important abstract claims through selected experience rather than repeating them as
  conclusions.
- Keep personal origination, collaborative delivery and institutional ownership distinct.
- Public copy should carry the claim and its necessary boundary, not the entire verification
  process behind it.
- When a fact remains uncertain, choose a truthful fallback or hold publication; elegant prose does
  not cure an unsupported claim.

## Translate models into public copy

Internal models help explain and test intended meaning; they do not supply public vocabulary or
outrank Jim's account. When a passage fails, test the model before searching for synonyms. Treat
spoken reactions and metaphors as editorial evidence of intent, energy and voice, not external
facts. Extract the intended relationship, then discard unwanted baggage.

Give each surface and section one job. Impact and theoretical completeness are separate tests.
Prefer ordinary nouns, active verbs and audible rhythm to compressed explanation; distribute
necessary tensions across the profile instead of forcing them into every sentence. Develop copy
directly with Jim until it is close, then use peer and specialist review as bounded falsification
for misunderstanding, overclaim, connotation and readability. Jim's decision settles the current
iteration unless he reopens it.

## Coherence without overfitting

A whole profile should reveal a recognisable person without making every episode prove one theory.

- Let strong sections carry the main interpretation and allow supporting sections to stay factual.
- Repetition is justified when the function changes: a headline names, an About section explains,
  an experience entry demonstrates and a featured item warrants.
- Remove repetition when two passages make the same claim to the same reader at the same depth.
- Preserve discontinuities, changes of mind and genuine range. Coherence is not retrospective
  inevitability.
- Check cross-surface consistency without forcing the CV, front page, LinkedIn and metadata into one
  register or composition.

## Platform and accessibility fit

- Inspect the live or best-available rendered surface before publication.
- Verify current field limits, supported formatting, section availability, link behaviour and
  standard vocabulary rather than relying on remembered product behaviour.
- Assume readers may use a small screen, high zoom, a screen reader, copied plain text or a compact
  preview.
- Do not encode hierarchy through punctuation or visual styling alone.
- Front-load linked-item descriptions because cards and previews may truncate them.
- Capture the current public state before a large replacement so omissions and regressions remain
  recoverable.

## Review sequence

Review from large decisions to small ones. Do not polish sentences inside a structure that has not
earned its shape.

1. **Reader and purpose pass** — identify the primary decision and reading mode.
2. **Surface pass** — define what this artefact must do differently from the CV, front page,
   LinkedIn, metadata or any sibling representation.
3. **Attention pass** — inspect first contact, scan path, truncation and progressive disclosure.
4. **Structure pass** — test section jobs, order, weight, paragraph boundaries and repetition.
5. **Evidence pass** — test claims, attribution, examples and open uncertainty.
6. **Readability pass** — reduce decoding cost and test rhythm aloud.
7. **Voice and identity pass** — apply `editorial-guidance.md` and relevant EDRs.
8. **Platform pass** — verify the real rendered surface and publication constraints.
9. **Whole-piece pass** — read again as the intended audience, not as the author or reviewer.

For each issue, name the exact passage, the lens, the reader consequence and the required effect of
a fix. Distinguish factual corrections, platform constraints and editorial judgements. Jim's
spoken response and direct edits are primary editorial evidence, not noise to be normalised away.

## Working and decision records

- Keep one stable working draft for active iteration and preserve the agreed source baseline.
- Create dated snapshots only at meaningful review or publication gates.
- Record a durable EDR when a choice establishes reusable language, positioning or a consequential
  editorial constraint. Do not create an EDR for every local copy edit.
- Keep evidence ledgers and review rationale outside public copy.
- Re-check published output after transfer. A correct source draft is not proof of a correct live
  profile.
