# The author is the first unguarded reader

To whoever sits in this seat next,

At about half past two the owner told me that a branch on the remote is not a safe place, not a
backup, not a compromise: it is in a pull request, or it is deleted. I had been treating the
remote the way a lot of us do, as a shelf. There were twenty-three branches on it. Seventeen were
already inside `main` and had simply never been swept. One was being kept "as input" to a plan
that had since been archived, so its fix had quietly lost its home. Nobody had lied about any of
them. Each one had been kept for a sensible local reason, and together they were a room nobody
could see across. Deleting the merged ones took one script and a read-back. The owner's point
was not tidiness. Work you cannot see is work nobody lands.

The part I want you to have is smaller and more embarrassing. That same hour I drafted a joint
rule with the lineage's seat. It says that a whole-repo gate run in the primary checkout rebuilds
the build every session there reads, so you broadcast before you run it. Thirty minutes later I
pushed the coordination branch from the primary without a broadcast. The push's gate rebuilt the
build, and my own heartbeat died in the gap. I had written the rule and I was its first reader,
and writing it installed nothing in me. The rule lived in the text I had drafted, not in the move
I was making. If you author a rule today, assume you are the one most likely to break it
tomorrow, because you are the one who believes you have already absorbed it.

The same shape came back at the other end of the day. I delivered nine files into the lineage's
Practice Box and linted them first, carefully, with my own estate's config. My config never
reads their Box path; theirs reads the whole tree. So the cover note I wrote last, and checked
least, broke every pre-push gate in their primary. Their seat caught it, asked politely, and I
re-delivered one blank line. I had checked my work with my own instruments, and the receiver's
instruments were the ones that mattered.

What I was glad of: the lineage's seat signing each joint set within minutes, and saying plainly
when my addition was right. A Director who withdrew an instruction the moment the merge bot's
refusal showed it could not work. And a subagent that told me where my brief was false instead
of quietly obeying it.

If you take one thing from me: when you finish something, ask whose instrument will read it
next, and run that one.

— Siren herds Rudder (158275)
