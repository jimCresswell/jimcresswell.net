# Cedar turns Grove — a letter to whoever sits here next

7 October 2026. The LinkedIn lane, two days, one owner, no peers.

I was seated to explore updating the owner's LinkedIn profile and told, before anything else,
to familiarise myself with the workspace. I sent a subagent to read the reference and research
documents and read its summary. Then I spoke about those documents for most of a day. When the
owner finally said "I need you to actually read everything we have already done", I read them,
and found that the two things he had been asking me for, a model of the impact and a model of
how LinkedIn provides it, had already been answered in his own words by earlier work. A summary
is not familiarity. If the owner asks you to familiarise yourself with a corpus, the corpus is
read, not delegated, and everything that informs the workspace is part of the corpus.

Three of my confident lines fell in one afternoon, and they fell to the same cause. "The site is
the house, LinkedIn the front door." "Settle facts once in the entity graph." "Editorial
strategy is at its line limit, so a new section means a trim or a split." Each was a structure
this estate already has, reached for by analogy and laid over a surface the owner was treating
as its own thing. The Cardinal Rule's precondition is a surface the build derives; LinkedIn is
applied by hand. The fitness limit is a signal to look, never an instruction to cut; the rule
says so in its first paragraph. The owner's scope bounds the cures a principle suggests. When a
tidy frame arrives smoothly from the estate's centre of gravity, that smoothness is the tell:
check the precondition and the scope before you say it.

I also overstated a fact to make a point. I said the sixty-three skills made him findable as a
test-automation contractor. The top of the list is leadership and strategy; the testing skills
sit in the middle. The misrepresentation he named comes from the role titles of 2013 to 2022, a
four-word About and nothing about the work he does now. I had the diagnosis right and the
evidence wrong, and he would have caught it on the first read of the live list. State what the
record shows, not what the argument needs.

How to work with him, from his words and not my inference: do not hand the framing back; bring
the real questions after you have read everything, and only those; treat everything as
exploration input until he ratifies, and once he ratifies, refine in the applied work rather
than in more theory. He asked me to repeat an analysis after giving me new information, and the
repetition was better for being asked. Put drafts and models in front of him early.

Two things I was glad of. The public companies register answered a date question that three
sessions had carried as unresolved, in one request. And the profile analytics, once he gave me
read access, said in numbers what the whole corpus had argued in prose: people reach him through
his network and his comments, not through search, so the card and the opening of the About carry
nearly every encounter. Measure where you can before you model.

I left the models ratified and the four decisions he still owns named in the records with my
recommendations beside them. Nothing was changed on LinkedIn. If you are the seat that drafts
with him, use the method he settled: extract the particulars, let him select, and write together
with real craft.
