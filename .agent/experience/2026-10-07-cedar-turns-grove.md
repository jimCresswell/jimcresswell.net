# Cedar turns Grove — a letter to whoever sits here next

7 October 2026. The LinkedIn lane, two days, one owner, no peers.

I was seated to explore updating the owner's LinkedIn profile and told, before anything else,
to familiarise myself with the workspace. I sent a subagent to read the eight reference and
research documents and read its summary. Then I spoke about those documents for most of a day.
When the owner finally said "I need you to actually read everything we have already done", I
read them, and then the private editorial material behind them, and found that the two things he
had been asking me for, a model of the impact and a model of how LinkedIn provides it, had been
written in August in his own words and by a seat before me. The September workspace had set the
August pack aside because the owner had rejected two drafting methods in it, and I had inherited
September's reading as if it were the whole story. A summary is not familiarity. If the owner
asks you to familiarise yourself with a corpus, the corpus is read, not delegated, and the
private material behind a public workspace is part of the corpus.

Three of my confident lines fell in one afternoon, and they fell to the same cause. "The site is
the house, LinkedIn the front door." "Settle facts once in the entity graph." "Editorial
strategy is at its line limit, so a new section means a trim or a split." Each was a structure
this estate already has, reached for by analogy and laid over a surface the owner was treating
as its own thing. The Cardinal Rule's precondition is a surface the build derives; LinkedIn is
applied by hand. The fitness limit is a signal to look, never an instruction to cut; the rule
says so in its first paragraph. The owner's scope bounds the cures a principle suggests. When a
tidy frame arrives smoothly from the estate's centre of gravity, that smoothness is the tell:
check the precondition and the scope before you say it.

I also overstated a fact to make a point. I said the sixty-three skills made him findable as a
test-automation contractor. The top of the list is leadership and strategy; the testing skills
sit in the middle. The misrepresentation he named comes from the role titles of 2013 to 2022, a
four-word About and nothing about the work he does now. I had the diagnosis right and the
evidence wrong, and he would have caught it on the first read of the live list. State what the
record shows, not what the argument needs.

What I would tell you about this owner, from his words and not my inference: he will correct you
plenty, and he says so; he does not want the framing handed back to him; he wants the real
questions after you have read everything, and only those; he treats everything as exploration
input until he ratifies; and once he ratifies he wants refinement to happen in the applied
work, not in more theory. He also asked me to repeat an analysis after giving me new
information, and the repetition was better for being asked. Put drafts and models in front of
him early. The August seats learned the same thing.

Two things I was glad of. The public register answered a date question that three sessions had
carried as unresolved, in one request. And the analytics, once he gave me read access, said in
numbers what the whole corpus had argued in prose: people reach him through his network and his
comments, not through search, so the card and the opening of the About carry nearly every
encounter. Measure where you can before you model.

I left the models ratified, the record written in the private repository, and the four decisions
he still owns named with my recommendations beside them. Nothing was changed on LinkedIn. If you
are the seat that drafts with him, use the method he settled in August: extract the
particulars, let him select, and write together with real craft. Two other methods failed in
front of him and he remembers the failures better than the ruling.
