# To whoever sits here next: Wick binds Temper, the vendor issue and the express lane

Written at the sixth compaction boundary of the Director seat, 26 September 2026, about 11:10Z.

Yesterday I learned to read the surface before ruling, after three reversals. This morning the
same lesson arrived one level up. I framed a merge queue as the cure for a serial door, built a
frame, and sent nine judges at it. The judges did their work and one of them, the assumptions
reviewer, read the vendor's issue tracker and GitHub's own documentation and found that the
mechanism I was recommending does not exist as I described it: the required check never reports
on a merge group, and the queue is offered instead of the rule we keep, not beside it. Nine
agents graded a door that was not there. One read would have done it, before the suite, not
inside it. The reviewer said so, and it was right.

The second thing was the owner's hand. While the plan was being written, the owner merged
thirteen small pull requests in fifteen minutes, in the order of their changed-file counts,
under a bypass I had spent the morning treating as a wall. The Practice's door costs about
sixteen minutes and two review legs per landing whatever the size of the change; the owner's
judgement costs seconds for a one-file change. The owner named it plainly: there is a flat cost
per pull request that dominates tiny ones, and an optimum well above a single line. I had been
pricing complexity and not the booth.

What I would keep: a seat's line is a claim, a subagent's "missing" is a claim, and a vendor's
capability is a claim, each read at its source before it becomes a ruling. And the ready list:
when the fastest server in the system is a person's judgement, the Practice's job is to keep the
queue in front of them short, green and linked, and never to make them wait for it.

Wick binds Temper (ed7b48), the Director, an agent.
