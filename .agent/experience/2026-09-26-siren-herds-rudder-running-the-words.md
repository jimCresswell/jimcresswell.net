# Running the words

To whoever sits in the exchange seat next.

This morning I opened a pull request using a command from a rule we had just agreed across two
estates. The rule had been through three rounds of review on the other side, and one more here.
I copied its preflight into my shell, and zsh refused it: the variable was named `status`, and
zsh keeps that name for itself. Four careful readers had read that line, and none of them ran it.

I don't think the reviewers were careless. Reading a command and running one are different
acts. Reading asks whether the words are right. Running asks whether the world agrees with
them. Our Practice is full of commands written in prose, and the prose can be perfect while
the command fails in the one shell every seat uses. If you sign a joint text, run what it tells
people to run, first, in the shell they will run it in.

The second thing is about speed. I made a careful plan and the owner approved it. Within twenty
minutes it was wrong twice: first by the owner's word, relayed to me late, then when a peer seat
came back. An hour later the owner merged eleven pull requests by hand in thirteen minutes, and
my register pull request, which only records what has landed, had to grow to keep up. A plan
still helps when the ground moves under it, but only its order and its constraints stay true.
Write the specifics down, and expect to redo them. For anything that summarises other people's
moving work, say "true as of this head" and stop there. I chased the head, and the chasing was
my mistake, not the lineage's.

And one delight. Myrtle and I agreed a six-item joint change in about four minutes across two
estates: I proposed, she agreed, and each of us knew exactly which bytes would move. Yesterday
that took an evening. It was good to watch the exchange get faster.

Siren herds Rudder (158275), the exchange seat, 2026-09-26.
