## 1. Opening record, authority and plan

The inquiry began by defining research questions, provisional hypotheses and competing frames before substantive execution. The initial distinctions, questions and seed sources were provisional inputs; their empirical claims were rechecked against original sources where accessible. The first phase was **P0: activate and ground the research**, not drafting personal copy.

Research start, cutoff and all access dates in this companion: **26 September 2026, UTC**. This is a purposive, source-backed desk review, not a registered systematic review or a statistical meta-analysis. The decision is how to understand and improve materials for consequential professional work, while bounding their likely effects. It is not whether Jim Cresswell's materials are good, nor how to write them.

Scope included employment, boards/advisory work, research, creative work, public appointments, collaboration and opportunity creation. Dependencies were commission → grounding → evidence map → appraisal and public-material inspection → critical synthesis → guidance → closure. Scope exclusions remained external outreach, publication, profile editing, personal application and participant experiments.

Open constructs at entry were exceptional effectiveness, useful curiosity, opportunity quality and the marginal effect of materials. The threshold, prestige/access and modest-returns explanations were retained as genuine alternatives. No acceptance criterion required proving the initial framing correct or proving that excellence causes exceptional career outcomes.

Completion required substantive dispositions for Q1–Q12; separate and connected CV/LinkedIn findings; H/B decisions; retained counterframes; inspectable evidence; context limits; conditional guidance; honest impact attribution; and saved, navigable deliverables. Search stopped when additional evidence would repeat an established warrant, or direct and contrary searches left a bounded gap. Inaccessible-source retries stopped at three legitimate retrieval attempts.

**Metacognitive changes.** At entry, the initial framing risked making an attractive opportunity-creation account the default explanation. After mapping, causal evidence was strongest for simpler interventions and relationships; opportunity creation was downgraded to a hypothesis. Before synthesis, confidence and curiosity were separated from accurate comprehension, and “a successful introduction” was separated from a workable contribution. The assumption that varied public profiles could be inspected deeply was revised after access failures. The corpus therefore supports stronger platform-mechanism findings than comparative LinkedIn composition findings.

**Free play, 09:52:28–09:53:06 UTC.** Material: contrasting CV extracts, Featured/search documentation, referral findings and cultural-matching findings. Capture: a short scratch note, reproduced here in substance. A relay suggested checking what survives forwarding; a museum-label analogy was discarded because it hid agency and unequal access. A maintenance note for a future collaborator suggested discussing enabling conditions; a productive refusal suggested measuring opportunity quality rather than volume. A “reputation engine” association was discarded because it made prestige self-validating. Retained seeds were challenged: forwarding does not establish advocacy effects; conditions often belong in conversation; refusal is useful only against actual goals. No association was counted as a finding. An empty harvest would also have satisfied the method.

## 4. Source register

All records accessed 26 September 2026. “Full text available; selected sections read” means the relevant methods/results/context were inspected, not that every appendix was audited. “Abstract” never warrants detailed risk-of-bias judgments. Source IDs are local to these two research documents.

### Experiments and empirical assessment

**R01 — Emma Wiles, Zanele Munyikwa and John J. Horton. _Algorithmic Writing Assistance on Jobseekers' Resumes Increases Hires_.** Published 2025, _Management Science_ 71(12), 10144–10164, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2024.04528). [Author manuscript, 7 March 2023](https://john-joseph-horton.com/papers/algo_writing_assistance.pdf). Family: writing-assistance experiment; versions and publicity count once. Depth: manuscript introduction, design, analysis and relevant results; final-publication metadata/abstract checked, final full text not appraised. Randomised 480,950 new platform entrants in 2021; primary approved/nonempty-profile analysis 194,701. Short profile résumés and nongenerative writing suggestions differ from senior CV editing. Hiring outcome supports L01; no observed employer-satisfaction loss is not proof of every dimension of matching quality. Post-treatment sample definition and unmeasured displacement constrain interpretation. Q2/Q4/Q11/Q12; H1/H7/H8; B1/B5/B6.

**R02 — Nature Human Behaviour / National Library of Medicine record. _Reducing discrimination against job seekers with and without employment gaps_.** 2022, _Nature Human Behaviour_. [PMC record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7614241/) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36471010/). Family: UK career-gap representation experiment. Depth: indexed abstract and selected indexed passage only; body retrieval blocked. Preregistered UK audit, N=9,022; representing experience as years rather than dates increased callbacks in the reported comparisons. A retrieved mechanism passage did not support an easier-reading explanation. This identifies a bounded format effect, not a recommendation to omit required dates. Detailed design robustness not independently appraised. Q2/Q4/Q9/Q10; H1/H6; B1/B5. L02.

**R03 — Laurel Wheeler, Robert Garlick, Eric Johnson, Patrick Shaw and Marissa Gargano. _LinkedIn(to) Job Opportunities: Experimental Evidence from Job Readiness Training_.** 2022, _American Economic Journal: Applied Economics_ 14(2), 101–125. [Publisher / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1257/app.20200025). Family: South African LinkedIn-training experiment. Depth: publisher abstract; linked full text required access. Randomised training, employment approximately seven percentage points higher, with reported persistence over twelve months. Joining/use/training are bundled; it does not estimate prose quality alone or establish effects for senior professionals. Q5/Q8/Q10/Q11; H1/H6; B2/B3/B5. L03.

**R04 — Karthik Rajkumar and colleagues. _A causal test of the strength of weak ties_.** 2022, _Science_. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abl4476) · [MIT institutional account](https://news.mit.edu/2022/weak-ties-linkedin-employment-0915). Family: LinkedIn People You May Know experiments; article and institutional accounts are one study, with platform involvement. Depth: indexed research abstract plus substantive MIT account; full paper inaccessible through attempted routes. Randomised connection recommendations and job mobility; structural and interaction measures, nonlinearity and industry differences matter. It does not test copy, posting or “always choose the weakest tie.” Q7/Q10/Q11; H4/H6; B3/B5. L04.

**R05 — Amanda Pallais and Emily Glassberg Sands. _Why the Referential Treatment? Evidence from Field Experiments on Referrals_.** 2016, _Journal of Political Economy_ 124(6), 1793–1828. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/688850) · [J-PAL manuscript, 6 July 2015](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/sites/default/files/research-paper/837_why_the_referential_treatment_Sands_July2015.pdf). Family: oDesk referral experiments; published and working versions one family. Depth: accessible manuscript introduction, experiment design and reported results; not a full reanalysis. Three 2013 experiments distinguish information, team production and peer influence; the last mechanism was not supported. Tasks and online workers differ from executive search. No document-quality manipulation. Q7/Q8/Q11; H4/H6; B3/B4/B5. L05.

**R06 — Lauren A. Rivera. _Hiring as Cultural Matching: The Case of Elite Professional Service Firms_.** 2012, _American Sociological Review_ 77(6), 999–1022. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0003122412463213) · [Accessible published PDF](https://edbatista.com/wp-content/uploads/files/hiring-as-cultural-matching-the-case-of-elite-professional-service-firms-rivera.pdf). Family: Rivera cultural-matching study. Depth: full text available; methods, argument, examples and limitations inspected. 120 interviews in 2006–08 and nine months of participant observation at one firm; graduate recruitment in elite U.S. law, consulting and banking. Observed deliberations, not interviews; mock résumés were discussion prompts, not a controlled factorial experiment. Cultural similarity complicates merit-based readings. Transfer to senior selection remains uncertain. Q1/Q4/Q7/Q9/Q10; H2/H5/H6/H8; B2/B3/B5. L06.

**R07 — Sonia K. Kang, Katherine A. DeCelles, András Tilcsik and Sora Jun. _Whitened Résumés: Race and Self-Presentation in the Labor Market_.** 2016, _Administrative Science Quarterly_ 61(3), 469–502. [Publisher / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0001839216639577). Family: résumé-whitening study. Depth: publisher abstract, not complete methods. Interviews, laboratory and audit components concern minority applicants and racial disclosure. Employer diversity language did not establish absence of discriminatory treatment. Supports caution about unequal evaluation, not a prescription to suppress identity. Q9/Q10; H6/H8; B5. L07.

**R08 — Christine L. Exley and Judd B. Kessler. _The Gender Gap in Self-Promotion_.** 2022, _Quarterly Journal of Economics_ 137(3), 1345–1381. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjac003) · [HCEO working-paper record, 2019](https://hceconomics.uchicago.edu/research/working-paper/gender-gap-self-promotion). Family: self-promotion experiments; working and published versions one family. Depth: institutional working-paper abstract and publication metadata; full text inaccessible. Self-evaluation differences conditional on performance in studied tasks challenge equating confidence with capability. No universal claim about genders or CV effects follows. Q1/Q4/Q9; H1/H6/H8; B1/B5. L07.

**R09 — Nicolas Roulin and Julia Levashina. _LinkedIn as a new selection method: Psychometric properties and assessment approach_.** Online 2018; 2019, _Personnel Psychology_ 72, 187–211. [Publisher / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/peps.12296). Family: LinkedIn assessment studies. Depth: publisher abstract and metadata. Two studies concern rating consistency, validity correlates and itemised versus global assessment. Profile length, pictures and connections correlate with favourable ratings; this does not make them causal editing prescriptions. Career-success indicators are not equivalent to prospective job performance. Detailed population and adverse-impact claims not generalised from the abstract. Q4/Q5/Q9/Q11; H1/H6; B1/B5. L08.

**R10 — David Aguado, José C. Andrés, Antonio L. Depth: methods and relevant results in full text. 615 employees of one company across twelve Spanish provinces, predominantly junior developers; coded profile variables related to employer performance/potential measures. Associations differed by subgroup and outcome. Profile completeness is not an intervention and cannot be said to improve work performance. Q5/Q10/Q11; H6; B1/B5. L08.

**R11 — Frédérique Bordignon, Lauranne Chaignon and Daniel Egret. _Promoting narrative CVs to improve research evaluation? Family: narrative-CV review; underlying studies not separately counted or reappraised. Depth: publisher abstract. Reviews 28 opinion pieces and seven experiments; questions the evidence connecting narrative form itself to promised benefits. Positive reception and procedural reform are not proof of causal improvement or fairness. Q3/Q4/Q9/Q10; H2/H6/H8; B1/B5/B6. L09.

**R12 — David R. Earnest, David G. Allen and Ronald S. Landis. _Mechanisms Linking Realistic Job Previews With Turnover: A Meta-Analytic Path Analysis_.** 2011, _Personnel Psychology_ 64(4), 865–897. Family: realistic-job-preview synthesis; constituent studies not separately appraised. Depth: institutional abstract. Several translations are deliberately blocked: pleasant reading → accurate understanding; perceived credibility → truth; callback → worthwhile opportunity; organisational outcome → sole individual contribution; documented platform affordance → effective owner tactic; individual selection gain → social benefit. These are different constructs, not stages that can be silently collapsed.

Population boundaries: R01/R05 involve online labour markets and comparatively short tasks/profiles; R03 a South African job-readiness programme; R06 graduate elite-service hiring; R10 largely junior ICT employees at one Spanish company. Professional advice reaches senior/board contexts but does not supply experimental comparisons. Public materials predominantly represent UK/U.S. contexts, including explicitly dated academic and creative genres. Evidence for other regions, languages, disability-specific encounters, non-public leadership work and local conventions is insufficient. No global norm about photographs, page limits or disclosure is asserted.
