# Exceptional CVs and LinkedIn presences: evidence, craft and consequential work

Version 1.0 · 26 September 2026

**Status:** commissioned desk research completed at the evidence boundary described below. Same-context self-review; no independent assurance. Research start and source cutoff: 26 September 2026.

[Evidence, claims and coverage companion](exceptional-cv-linkedin-evidence-companion-2026-09-26.md)

## 1. What the research establishes

Exceptionally effective materials would help the right people understand consequential work unusually well, judge its relevance with appropriate confidence, and take worthwhile next steps. This is a defensible **design ambition**, not an empirically validated formula or a statistical percentile. Exceptional capability, prestigious employment, accomplished presentation and the additional effect of the materials are four different things.

The strongest direct evidence favours some familiar fundamentals. A large randomised writing-assistance study found increased hiring among new entrants to an online work platform. The intervention improved basic expression; it did not test exquisite executive CVs against already competent ones. Its working manuscript reports an approximately 8% relative hiring increase, with a 95% interval of 3–13%, and improvements in writing concentrated among initially weaker writers [R01]. A UK field experiment also found that changing how experience was represented affected callbacks [R02; abstract-level access]. These findings make “presentation never matters” untenable. They do not establish how much frontier refinement matters for consequential professionals.

Relationships and institutional selection are substantial alternative explanations for success. Experiments on LinkedIn connections and referrals concern access, information and collaboration, rather than copy quality [R04–R05]. Qualitative hiring research shows how cultural similarity can influence evaluation [R06]. A successful person's elegant CV is therefore an example of a choice, not evidence that the choice caused success.

Three conclusions guide the practical advice:

1. **Remove preventable obstacles first.** Accurate facts, relevant evidence, legible structure, usable files and accurate platform fields have clearer warrants than elaborate personal branding. Some warrants are experimental; others are documented system behaviour or professional practice. They should not be conflated.
2. **Pursue excellence where misunderstanding would be costly.** Ambiguous authority, collective achievements, preventive work, career transitions and unfamiliar expertise can justify unusually careful selection and explanation. The advanced craft proposed here is a reasoned response to those problems, supported by practice and contrasting examples. Its marginal career effect remains unquantified.
3. **Evaluate usefulness beyond conversion.** Better understanding, calibrated confidence, useful questions, suitable opportunities and eventual contribution require different observations. More attention or more offers can coexist with poorer mutual selection.

The corpus is mainly Anglophone and weighted towards hiring. South African employment research and Spanish ICT research test some transfer assumptions, but public examples chiefly represent UK/U.S. engineering, scholarship and visual art. Five actual CVs were inspectable in substantive text; three LinkedIn records yielded only partial indexed traces. There was no complete paired CV/profile inspection, private executive-file access or observed hiring process. Those limits particularly constrain conclusions about profile composition, opportunity creation and long-term effects.

## 2. What excellence means—and what it cannot mean here

For this inquiry, “consequential” describes situations in which expertise, judgement or leadership can materially affect outcomes. It does not designate a social elite. A research method, reliable public service, preserved institution or avoided failure may matter more than a large budget or impressive title. The same person may make a major contribution in one environment and have little useful authority in another.

The professional, an adviser, a prospective collaborator and a selection committee can reasonably want different things. A professional may value autonomy, sustainable conditions or a change of direction. An evaluator may need to distinguish candidates under time pressure. A community may care about fair access and whether useful work happens. A report that optimises only invitations would conceal these differences.

An excellent representation should aspire to do more than pass a competence check. It can expose the difficult choice inside an achievement, convey a recognisable way of working, make an unfamiliar contribution intelligible, and offer enough substance to warrant a serious conversation. It need not do all these things on every surface. These are proposed quality dimensions, not a validated scoring instrument:

| Dimension                   | Beyond competent execution                                           | What would count against it                                       |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Relevance                   | Shows why a distinctive contribution could matter in this situation. | Readers admire the record but cannot identify a useful role.      |
| Explanatory precision       | Makes the problem, decision, personal role and limits intelligible.  | Greater detail increases confidence without improving accuracy.   |
| Evidential depth            | Gives a credible route from claim to appropriately selected support. | Links are impressive but do not substantiate the claim.           |
| Human interest              | Offers something worth thinking or asking about.                     | Warmth or novelty crowds out relevant information.                |
| Transferability             | Helps a reader see both applicability and conditions of success.     | A past result is represented as a context-free personal property. |
| Usability across encounters | Remains understandable when skimmed, forwarded or discussed.         | Meaning depends on the author explaining every phrase.            |

No reference population, common outcome or counterfactual permits a “top 1%” claim. Nor can the review rank these dimensions universally. A prescribed public-appointment application, academic CV and unsolicited advisory introduction are different tasks [R14–R16]. Excellence may consist of fulfilling a disciplined genre exceptionally well, rather than escaping it.

## 3. Fundamentals, status and the uncertain frontier

The threshold explanation deserves priority: many materials fail because the reader cannot locate the relevant experience, understand the prose or tell what the person actually did. Fixing those defects may account for much observable improvement. R01 supports a bounded version of that account; it does not establish a universal threshold or prove that further refinement has zero value.

The status explanation also survives. Recognised institutions, prior relationships and reputational signals can supply information or grant access before anyone reads closely. Rivera's study concerns graduate recruitment into elite professional services, not appointments of senior leaders; it nevertheless undermines the assumption that selection is simply an impartial reading of work evidence [R06]. It would be equally mistaken to conclude that every referral is empty favouritism: referral experiments found information about workers that observable records did not capture [R05].

The modest-returns explanation remains unresolved. The review found no suitable causal comparison isolating intensive refinement of already strong CVs or LinkedIn profiles for the target population. Neither practitioner confidence nor attractive examples fill that gap. A recent commercial application experiment reported differences between human and AI-generated résumés, but changed job pools and timing across sequential conditions; it cannot isolate authorship or editorial quality [R21]. It does not rebut the randomised assistance study, which tested a different intervention.

This leaves a conditional case for advanced craft. Its strongest rationale is a specific, consequential communication problem: an unfamiliar achievement, a mistaken inference about seniority, hidden judgement behind a team result, or uncertainty about a transition. “Make it more impressive” is a weaker brief. A rare valuable opportunity can justify substantial effort, but its rarity makes return estimation harder; it does not licence an invented expected payoff.

## 4. Selecting and presenting consequential evidence

Begin with a private inventory of real work, then select for the reader's decision. Selection is not fabrication. A complete career archive and an effective first encounter have different information requirements. Executive-search advice favours relevant accomplishment and context [R13]; UKRI's research résumé explicitly broadens recognised contribution beyond outputs alone [R15]. These are distinct practice and institutional warrants, not proof that a particular wording causes better appointments.

For a significant example, ask:

- What problem, constraint or uncertainty existed, and why did it matter?
- What did this person notice, decide, originate, influence or implement?
- What authority did they hold, and which decisions belonged to others?
- What did the team or institution achieve, over what period?
- What supports the account, and what cannot reasonably be attributed to this person?
- What conditions would need to recur for the experience to be relevant elsewhere?

Not every answer belongs in public prose. Keep the qualification that prevents a material misunderstanding close to the claim; put secondary detail in a suitable supporting object. This is a synthesis principle, not a demand for independent corroboration of every professional statement.

**Constructed contrasts—not real people, measured outcomes or suggested personal copy:**

| Weak or misleading representation                    | More informative alternative                                                                                                                                                                                 | Trade-off to preserve                                                                                                              |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| “Visionary leader who transformed the organisation.” | “When service demand outgrew the operating model, I developed the options and recommended staged migration. The executive team approved the approach; I led implementation with operations and engineering.” | More context costs space. Select the decision that matters, rather than narrating the whole project.                               |
| “Delivered the company's revenue growth.”            | “Led the pricing analysis and rollout within a wider commercial programme. Revenue rose during the period; sales expansion and market conditions also contributed.”                                          | A reader still needs the scale and evidence if these are available. Qualification should sharpen attribution, not dissolve agency. |
| “Prevented a catastrophic outage.”                   | “Identified a failure mode in recovery procedures, secured time to address it and introduced rehearsed failover. Subsequent exercises met the recovery objective.”                                           | Observed controls and tests support preparedness; they do not prove what catastrophe would otherwise have occurred.                |
| “Maintained the platform.”                           | “Kept the service dependable through staff turnover by documenting operational knowledge, training new owners and reducing unresolved failure modes.”                                                        | Add appropriate evidence if available. Do not invent incidents avoided or savings to make maintenance look heroic.                 |
| “Expert in everything needed for the next role.”     | “Established experience in X; applied it to Y with specialist partners; now seeking work where that combination is useful.”                                                                                  | Preserve ambition while separating demonstrated experience from intended direction.                                                |

Judgement is often visible in the alternatives considered, the constraint respected or the decision changed. A list of outcomes alone can hide it. Conversely, elaborate reasoning can obscure whether anything happened. Combine one meaningful decision with an appropriate result or learning, rather than attaching an essay to every bullet.

Supporting evidence should answer an actual doubt. A technical talk may show explanation and technical reasoning; a publication may establish a particular research contribution; a portfolio may make craft inspectable. None automatically proves leadership across contexts. Confidential work can be described at an authorised level of abstraction. The absence of a public artifact is not evidence that the work lacked value.

## 5. CV craft: a controlled document for a particular use

A CV can be tailored, exported, forwarded and read without its author present. Start by identifying whether it is a screening résumé, comprehensive academic record, board document, public professional account or required component of a structured application. Do not make one file perform every job.

A practical default for an unconstrained professional CV is an identifiable professional direction, selected relevant experience, sufficient chronology and supporting qualifications or work. Order should answer the reader's most important uncertainty. Education-first may suit one academic or early-career use; experience-first may suit another. Length follows the task and any explicit instructions. Spencer Stuart's board guidance is more restrictive than its general leadership-story advice [R13–R14]; academic and creative records further defeat a universal page rule [C03–C05].

Make the document navigable at more than one depth. Headings, meaningful opening clauses and selective detail should permit orientation before close reading. Keep essential claims self-contained: a forwarded PDF may be printed or separated from its links. Use links to extend evidence, not to require scavenger hunting for the basic account.

File usability is a separate test from prose quality. Greenhouse documents particular parsing failures and explains that unsuccessful parsing need not remove the attached résumé [R19]. W3C describes correct PDF reading order [R20]. The resulting conservative submission practice is to use a simple structure, ordinary section labels and real text, follow the receiving system's format requirements, and inspect extracted information where possible. A single column is a defensible fallback for an unknown parser, not a law that every multi-column document is unreadable. Accessibility and extraction are related but distinct: a well-tagged PDF can have correct assistive reading order while a particular parser still mishandles it.

Craft should help a reader retain the right thing. A coherent example is a plausible aid to memory, but this review does not establish a universal seconds-to-read rule, font formula or causal memorability recipe. Do not compress an important qualification out of existence to meet folklore about attention spans.

## 6. LinkedIn: discovery, interpretation and ongoing presence

LinkedIn adds structured fields, search products, relationships, activity and visibility controls to the professional account. Its public profile is not merely a CV with extra room. It can meet people before a role exists and be revisited after an introduction. These are affordances; their existence does not establish the benefit of any particular profile strategy.

The current documentation supports a concrete distinction. Recruiter filters use fields such as companies, location, experience and skills, while keyword search can inspect profile text [R17a]. A February 2026 LinkedIn preprint also describes semantic retrieval and model-based ranking for named search experiences [R18]. It does not expose a complete universal ranking formula or validate a keyword-density tactic. Accurate conventional terminology and intelligible descriptions are therefore sensible; keyword stuffing, title inflation and claims to “beat the algorithm” are not justified.

Treat profile components as complementary:

| Component                          | Proposed job                                                           | Conditional choice and failure test                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Headline and initial visible text  | Help an unfamiliar reader recognise a relevant professional direction. | Use recognisable function and substantive specialism where useful. A highly distinctive slogan may fail to identify the work; a generic title may hide a valuable combination. Check what can be inferred without opening everything.           |
| About                              | Orient the reader to work, interests and reasons to explore further.   | Offer a clear account and one or two substantive points of interest. Avoid a compulsory life story or an abstract identity manifesto. Ask whether the reader knows what to examine next.                                                        |
| Experience                         | Establish a credible record with context and personal contribution.    | Keep facts consistent with the CV; adapt depth and ordering to a broader audience. Repeated marketing summaries can leave authority and chronology unclear.                                                                                     |
| Skills and other structured fields | Supply truthful searchable information.                                | Include relevant terms actually supported by experience. More entries are not automatically better evidence or better ranking.                                                                                                                  |
| Featured and linked work           | Provide inspectable depth for interested readers.                      | Choose evidence that answers a likely question. Featured content is not itself discoverable through search according to current documentation, and viewing requires sign-in [R17b]. An essential explanation needs a more accessible route too. |
| Recommendations                    | Add a situated account from someone else.                              | Treat them as attributed testimony, not certification. Relationship and specificity can affect credibility judgments [R22]; this does not establish truth or future performance.                                                                |
| Activity                           | Let relevant thinking or work become encounterable over time.          | A useful contribution may create a reason to visit the profile. No posting frequency or engagement target is established here. Volume can consume time without improving opportunity quality.                                                   |

For example, a **constructed** headline such as “Engineering leader working on dependable public services” gives a functional anchor; “Changing the world through visionary innovation” leaves the work unclear. The first may still be too broad for a specialist search and too narrow for a genuine transition. The choice should follow actual experience and desired conversations, not a fixed formula.

Open to Work visibility is a practical choice about exposure. The documented recruiter-only setting includes limitations: LinkedIn does not guarantee complete privacy [R17c]. Use the setting that fits the person's circumstances; the review does not recommend signalling availability for everyone.

Preserve continuity across time without inventing inevitability. A professional can explain a new direction while leaving earlier work accurately described. Review stale claims, role dates, links and availability when circumstances change. The current documentation was checked on 26 September 2026; no authenticated profile interface or search-ranking experiment was run.

## 7. Human interest, discovery and the seams between encounters

Human interest need not mean a personal anecdote. It can arise from an unexpected problem, a precise observation, a consequential trade-off or a piece of work worth examining. A reader may want to know why a seemingly obvious approach was rejected. That question can be more useful than undifferentiated admiration.

“Useful curiosity” remains a hypothesis. A follow-up question may reflect reader expertise, confusion or politeness rather than superior materials. Likewise warmth and cultural similarity may affect liking without improving assessment [R06]. Personal interests can be appropriate, but evidence of selection bias is not an instruction to imitate elite leisure or conceal identity.

The encounters are not a reliable linear funnel. An introduction may precede profile viewing; a committee may see a CV without LinkedIn; a published work may create contact before either. The following seams identify distinct jobs and likely losses:

| Seam                                | What needs to survive                                          | Typical failure                                                                 | Useful diagnostic—not an executed test                                               |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Discovery → profile or CV           | Enough accurate relevance to justify attention.                | Search visibility brings the wrong audience; a label is mistaken for expertise. | Compare the types of enquiries with intended work, not just view counts.             |
| First reading → supporting evidence | A clear question and a relevant evidence route.                | Dead links, sign-in barriers or impressive but irrelevant artifacts.            | Can the support actually resolve the uncertainty raised by the claim?                |
| Reading → introduction              | A concise, accurate reason for connecting these people.        | A distinctive record becomes generic praise.                                    | Can a willing intermediary explain relevance without inventing an achievement?       |
| Introduction → collective decision  | Context, attribution and limits alongside the recommendation.  | A trusted advocate's confidence substitutes for shared examination.             | What information reached the committee, and what changed its judgment?               |
| Selection → agreement about work    | A realistic understanding of role, authority and dependencies. | An appointment is accepted on incompatible assumptions.                         | Compare the expected contribution with decision rights, resources and collaborators. |
| Agreement → actual contribution     | Conditions under which the work can succeed.                   | Later results are attributed entirely to the original materials or individual.  | Reconstruct decisions and conditions over time, including contrary evidence.         |

Network experiments establish that changing connection opportunities can affect job mobility, with important differences by tie measure and industry [R04]. They do not show that making a profile more interesting has the same effect. LinkedIn training research found employment gains in South Africa, but the intervention bundled participation and use; it does not isolate profile prose [R03].

Materials may be useful to an advocate as a memory aid, explanation or checkable account. Their marginal contribution to accurate advocacy is not established by referral research. A trusted colleague's direct knowledge could dominate the document, and the document may become relevant only when the colleague introduces someone to a less familiar decision-maker. This is an important conditional use case, not a demonstrated average effect.

## 8. Opportunity creation, mutual selection and conditions for good work

An existing vacancy supplies a specification. A new collaboration, advisory role or reshaped remit may instead emerge when someone recognises a relevant combination of experience and need. The materials could contribute by making that combination intelligible. No retrieved study isolates this mechanism for high-consequence professionals. Successful biographies and retrospective origin stories would not be sufficient proof.

The practical hypothesis is to make a plausible contribution discussable without pretending that every future possibility is already proven. A concise account of problems someone has worked on can invite a conversation broader than a job title. It can also become so broad that no one knows when to call. Maintain a recognisable anchor and distinguish established experience from exploratory interest.

Mutual selection includes learning that a role is unsuitable. An apparent success can be a poor opportunity if it lacks authority, resources, complementary expertise or sustainable conditions. Conversely, a smaller or less prestigious role may enable more valuable work. Title and compensation alone cannot settle quality.

Materials can introduce these issues through truthful examples of how work was accomplished. The detailed negotiation usually belongs later. A public profile need not become a list of demands. A **constructed** statement such as “My most effective projects paired domain expertise with teams able to act on the findings” can open a question about implementation; it does not guarantee a supportive future setting.

Research on realistic job previews concerns information supplied by employers and turnover, not candidate-authored CVs changing a remit [R12]. It is adjacent support for examining expectations, with a reversed direction of communication. It does not establish a causal bridge from opportunity to contribution.

Future validation should trace who noticed what, whether the material changed their understanding, and whether that change affected the work agreed. It should permit a finding that the document merely confirmed a decision made through relationships. Neither this commission nor this report authorises contacting participants to collect those accounts.

## 9. Context, identity and unequal evaluation

The principle “show relevant evidence clearly” travels more easily than a template. Context changes what evidence means, who reads it and what decisions are possible.

| Context                    | What the materials may need to enable                                                              | Why transfer can fail                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Executive or advisory work | Understand decisions, authority, organisational context and relevance to a particular problem.     | Senior titles obscure differences in mandate; private relationships may dominate access.                                     |
| Board selection            | Assess a contribution to collective oversight and a particular board's needs [R14].                | Operating achievement is not automatically evidence of effective governance.                                                 |
| Academic and research work | Inspect a disciplinary record or contributions requested by a funder [R15, C03].                   | Publication lists and narrative résumés serve different assessment rules; narrative reform is not proven bias removal [R11]. |
| Creative practice          | Encounter work, commissions, exhibitions and development of practice [C04–C05].                    | Corporate impact bullets can erase the object of evaluation: the work itself.                                                |
| UK public appointments     | Respond to specified criteria and application components [R16].                                    | An elegant general CV cannot replace required evidence in a supporting statement.                                            |
| Technical work             | Explain enough for general evaluators while preserving inspectable specialist substance [C01–C02]. | Either jargon or excessive simplification can hide the relevant contribution.                                                |
| Career transition          | Make a credible connection between past evidence and a new setting.                                | A seamless narrative can overstate transfer; an exhaustive chronology can hide it.                                           |

These are use distinctions, not regional rules for all people. U.S. commercial résumé conventions must not be imposed on comprehensive academic CVs; UK application instructions can prescribe a different artifact. The review does not establish local photo, personal-detail or length conventions globally.

Disclosure is an exercise of agency. Truthfulness does not require exhaustive personal disclosure. Research on résumé whitening and self-promotion shows why presentation and confidence should not be treated as neutral measurements of ability [R07–R08; abstract-level appraisal]. Evidence of unequal treatment should inform evaluators' scrutiny of their own procedures. It does not create an obligation for affected professionals to suppress identity, disclose sensitive circumstances or perform a more privileged persona.

Evaluators can use explicit criteria, distinguish observed work from impression, and seek structured evidence before treating polished presentation as competence. Those are reasoned safeguards here; this review has not experimentally compared a complete evaluation procedure.

## 10. What actual public materials can—and cannot—teach

The sample was deliberately varied, not representative or ranked. It contains five substantive CV texts and three partial LinkedIn records, covering seven people because one person appears on both surfaces. These are observations of published representations, not independent checks of career claims. No biography was substituted for a CV. Visual typography was not reliably inspected; the analysis concerns textual structure and content.

| Case                             | Observed choice                                                                                                                                      | Interpretation and boundary                                                                                                       |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| C01 infrastructure engineer's CV | A one-page record combines infrastructure work, collaboration, mentoring and selected talks.                                                         | Compactness can preserve several forms of contribution. No evidence establishes its effect on hiring.                             |
| C02 technologist's CV            | A one-page, education-first record gives different levels of detail across roles and selected publications; older publication-status markers remain. | Concision and freshness are separate dimensions. An undated public file must not be treated as a verified current career account. |
| C03 academic CV, June 2022       | Eleven pages separate scholarship, teaching, advising and further professional activities.                                                           | A comprehensive disciplinary record has a different information job from a screening résumé. The explicit date bounds its use.    |
| C04 artist's full CV             | Categorised exhibitions and related records connect to work; some “recent” labelling covers older periods.                                           | The evidence object and the record can complement each other. Status cannot validate composition, and labels can age.             |
| C05 artist's CV                  | A chronological account includes exhibitions, commissions and other professional activity.                                                           | This offers a further creative-practice contrast without inferring capability or success from public prominence.                  |

The sample does not justify a “best profiles” gallery. It does show why a single ideal length, sequence or narrative style is a poor starting assumption. Source URLs, access depth, observation/interpretation separation and case-specific hypotheses are in the companion.

## 11. Conditional guidance and when to stop

The following principles translate the evidence and reasoning into decisions. “Documented” means the feature or procedure is described in a primary source, not that its use improves careers. “Synthesis” means a practical inference requiring evaluation in context.

| Principle and intended benefit                      | Use when / competing choice                                                                                                           | Failure mode                                                                   | Assess usefulness                                                                                  | Warrant                                                              |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Repair correctness and comprehension first.         | There are errors, ambiguous claims or missing essentials. Further stylistic refinement competes with this work.                       | Fluent rewriting changes meaning or increases unsupported certainty.           | Compare the claims and reader interpretations with the underlying facts.                           | Bounded experiment R01; synthesis beyond its population.             |
| Select evidence for the actual decision.            | A reader needs to judge a particular contribution. A comprehensive record may be required in other genres.                            | Tailoring erases relevant limits or invents fit.                               | Can the reader locate evidence for the decision's important criteria?                              | Practice R13; institutional procedures R15–R16.                      |
| Explain judgement and attribution.                  | Outcomes depend on teams, constraints or preventive work. A short factual entry may suffice for familiar credentials.                 | Context overwhelms the main point, or qualifications erase real agency.        | Can someone distinguish personal action, authority and collective result?                          | Practice plus author synthesis; no direct frontier effect estimate.  |
| Layer orientation and inspectable depth.            | Several reader types need different detail. A mandated single artifact may constrain layering.                                        | Essential meaning is hidden behind links or inaccessible objects.              | Read the core alone, then test whether support answers the relevant question.                      | System constraints R17b, R19–R20; descriptive cases.                 |
| Make interest substantive.                          | A question or example can reveal relevant thinking. Direct factual brevity may be better for routine screening.                       | Novelty, hobbies or mystery obscure the work or invite similarity bias.        | What specific question arises, and is it useful to the intended decision?                          | Hypothesis; contrary qualitative evidence R06.                       |
| Make truthful discoverability a separate task.      | Search-mediated encounters matter. Introductions may be the dominant route elsewhere.                                                 | Optimisation language exceeds demonstrated experience.                         | Inspect actual fields and, when authorised, the relevance of resulting enquiries.                  | Current documentation R17a; vendor technical account R18.            |
| Help an advocate preserve the reason for relevance. | Materials will be forwarded or discussed. Direct knowledge may already suffice.                                                       | Generic praise replaces checkable information; caveats disappear.              | Compare an authorised reader's retelling with the source claim.                                    | Hypothesis; adjacent, distinct relationship evidence R05 and R22.    |
| Support mutual selection and discuss conditions.    | A role or collaboration is ambiguous. Prescribed screening may require narrower content initially.                                    | Aspirations look like proven capability; public requirements sound inflexible. | Do conversations resolve authority, resources and expectations?                                    | Hypothesis; adjacent R12 and practice R14.                           |
| Preserve agency in disclosure.                      | Personal context is relevant or the person wants it included. Omission may be appropriate.                                            | Bias is normalised as a personal branding problem.                             | Has the professional chosen the disclosure, and can evaluators justify its relevance?              | R07–R08 plus normative reasoning.                                    |
| Stop when the next change lacks a concrete purpose. | The core is accurate, usable and relevant, with important ambiguities resolved. Continue if a consequential misunderstanding remains. | Endless refinement substitutes for work, relationships or evidence creation.   | Name the expected change in understanding and the cost of testing it; stop if neither is credible. | Proportionality and unresolved H7, not a measured universal optimum. |

For an already strong document, the next valuable action may be producing a demonstrable work sample, doing the underlying work well, learning about an institution's needs or having an authorised professional conversation. This review cannot rank those investments for every person. It does establish that rewriting is not the only relevant lever.

Stopping is provisional, not neglect. Reopen the materials when facts change, the audience changes, an important misunderstanding is observed, a source of evidence becomes available or a platform requirement materially changes. Maintain a reliable core and adapt specific views; avoid a proliferation of inconsistent versions.

## 12. Assessing usefulness and attributing effects over time

Keep separate observations for different outcomes:

| Outcome               | A possible observation                                                                     | What it does not establish                                     |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Comprehension         | A reader accurately explains the work, role and limits.                                    | Liking, appointment or future performance.                     |
| Calibrated confidence | The reader identifies what is supported and what needs checking.                           | Truth merely because the reader feels certain.                 |
| Useful curiosity      | Questions concern a relevant decision, problem or collaboration.                           | General interest or a career effect.                           |
| Opportunity quality   | Enquiries fit the professional's goals and have credible scope and conditions.             | That materials caused those enquiries.                         |
| Mutual selection      | Both parties resolve important expectations, including a reasoned decision not to proceed. | That every refusal is beneficial.                              |
| Eventual contribution | Work produces relevant outcomes under recorded conditions.                                 | That the original profile, CV or individual alone caused them. |

For later authorised application, establish the intended audience and outcome before editing. Preserve the baseline and record substantive changes. A small purposive reader review can identify misunderstanding and missing evidence; it is not a representative estimate or independent assurance merely because several people participate. Do not use simulated readers as empirical human evidence.

For observed live outcomes, record exposure where available, opportunity type, source of introduction, timing, market conditions and simultaneous changes. A before/after rise in views or invitations is confounded by activity, network changes, availability and demand. With rare opportunities, detailed process tracing may be more informative than a conversion-rate dashboard, but retrospective accounts still have recall and attribution limits.

A controlled comparison would require its own authorised design, suitable outcomes and attention to interference between candidates or network contacts. None was conducted. Individual gains may displace others or increase presentation costs; the corpus does not establish a net societal return from more intensive polishing. Fairer assessment and better use of capability remain aims, not automatic consequences of better prose.

## 13. Completion and remaining uncertainty

P0–P6 are complete as bounded desk research. All Q1–Q12 have substantive dispositions; H1–H8 and B1–B6 are individually tested or explicitly left uncertain in the companion. The ten acceptance criteria are satisfied at that scope, including visible gaps and honest self-review. This is not a claim of exhaustive coverage, causal superiority, independent assurance or validated personal advice.

Established within specified settings: expression can affect hiring; relationships can add information and alter access; evaluators and institutions do not treat every representation neutrally; named platforms impose concrete discovery and access constraints. Stronger general conclusions concern distinctions and disciplined practice, not universal effect sizes.

Still uncertain: the incremental return to frontier refinement, whether material-induced curiosity improves opportunities, how much materials change advocacy or create a remit, and whether any subsequent gains improve eventual contribution or collective welfare. Full LinkedIn composition and cross-cultural transfer are under-observed in this corpus. These gaps narrow conclusions rather than leave the commission indefinitely open.

No personal assessment, outreach, participant experiment, profile change or publication formed part of the research.
