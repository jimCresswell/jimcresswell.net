## About

Status: approved (Jim, 7 October 2026; revised by Jim, 10 October 2026; the readers' line pass applied, awaiting Jim)

I like problems that don't have a shape yet.

Physics research taught me that: studying the early Universe, where understanding is something you build, assess, and let reality overturn. When I moved into technology I kept the method and changed the aim. Explaining a system is satisfying. Changing one is better.

In the spring of 2020 Oak National Academy was a few months old, an emergency response to school closures that became a new kind of national public service. I joined to get the first teacher-facing service over the line and stayed to shape the engineering strategy behind the whole thing. The hardest call was arguing to rebuild the emergency platform while it was still what kept lessons online. Four years on, the rebuild is what everything new is built on.

The last few years have been driven by one idea: Oak's curriculum should be wherever teachers already are, including inside the AI tools they use. I conceived the Oak AI plugin and built it, from an open SDK on Oak's curriculum data to the service that now puts a fully sequenced and resourced, openly licensed curriculum into ChatGPT and Claude. The immediate impacts are something I care deeply about, but the higher-order impacts, what other people build once innovation is cheap, are where the biggest changes are made.

To build it, I first built the way of working: the Practice, through which humans and AI agents engineer together safely at pace and scale. It learns as it works and keeps safe the institutional knowledge that AI-first work puts at risk. I use it every day. It is how the plugin got made.

Before Oak: a consultancy of my own, the FT Web App, testing and delivery practice for British Airways and the UK Passport Office, and HP Labs. The more I learn, the bigger the questions get.

### Principal Engineer · Oak National Academy · June 2022 – Present · United Kingdom

Status: drafted (8 October 2026; revised by Jim, 10 October 2026; the readers' line pass applied, awaiting Jim)

Oak's engineering was four contractors when I arrived in 2020. It is now six product squads and three platform groups, and I have carried its strategy throughout: the long-term technical direction, what gets built and what does not, and the case for it to engineering and non-engineering senior leadership. I helped define the function as it grew, from its first QA engineer and first engineering manager to its first platform group to a Team Topologies design for the cross-functional teams.

I made the emergency platform safe to change before anything else. When change still got harder and releases slower, I argued that optimising it was the wrong starting point and that Oak should rebuild. Oak did; I stood behind the call and accepted its near-term delivery and reputational risk. Everything Oak has built in the four years since stands on that rebuild.

The Oak AI plugin began in 2024 as an opportunity I saw in Oak's curriculum, sequenced end to end, written by curriculum experts and published under the Open Government Licence: any AI assistant could reach it, if someone built the route. I made the case, built a public SDK on the data by hand before the route existed, then the route itself, a service now in ChatGPT, Claude and any assistant that supports Model Context Protocol (MCP). The curriculum is the experts' work; the plugin's shape, its development and its strategy came from me. As it became a product the question changed, from what was possible to how Oak's values would live in it. The answer is that the standards travel with the data. The plugin carries the curriculum principles and the accessibility bar the lessons were written to, and an assistant building on Oak builds on them too.

The way of working came before the plugin was a product. While the plugin was still an SDK, I was building a Practice for software engineering with AI agents at pace and scale, in which every decision is recorded with its reasons, every agent works to the same written rules, and what one session learns the next one keeps. Inside it the agents build; the judgement and the calls are mine. Practice and plugin alike are public, in Oak's Open Curriculum Ecosystem repository.

Zero to one is complete. The plugin has its own cross-discipline product squad now, building out the direction I set, and that is the pattern: build the first version and the way of working, hand them on, and go where the next problem is still unformed.
