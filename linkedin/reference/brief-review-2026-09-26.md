## Consequential assumptions and gaps

### 1. Desirable outcomes have not become a usable priority

A profile can serve several legitimate aims, and they can favour different choices. A familiar occupational label can aid recognition while giving an incomplete account of the work; a distinctive phrase can invite some readers and lose others.

**Question:** What should a well-matched person understand, and what conversation should that understanding make possible? What attractive-looking opportunity would actually be a poor fit?

**Consequence:** Without an answer, the assistant may substitute its own preference for prestige, recruitment or narrative elegance. A ranking aspiration without a reference population or an assessable threshold cannot guide those choices, even when labelled non-statistical.

**Smallest resolution:** Agree a provisional primary reader decision and the most important misunderstanding to avoid. Preserve other readers as secondary tests. A vacancy, fixed future title or complete career strategy is unnecessary. This is a consequential owner choice, not a reason to delay factual inspection. Priority: high; confidence in the textual gap: high.

### 2. Correcting an engineering-only reading could preselect a new hierarchy

Guarding against an engineering-only reading is right, and so is preserving Jim's account of creating and delivering a user-facing service. Directing attention again and again towards service creation, origination, leadership and method authorship can nevertheless preselect a new hierarchy.

**Question:** Which of those distinctions helps a reader understand a concrete contribution, and which risks becoming another abstract identity to demonstrate?

Engineering and service creation are not mutually exclusive categories. Current title, scope of responsibility, personal contribution, exercised judgement and desired future work are different dimensions. A broader contribution need not be described through a grander title.

**Smallest resolution:** Begin from episodes of work: the problem recognised, decisions made, service brought into use, collaborators and subsequent use. Then test several descriptions against that material. Keep “the existing title is accurate, but the supporting account is incomplete” as a serious possibility. This protects agency without treating a rhetorical move away from engineering as the success test. Priority: high; confidence in the anchoring risk: moderate.

### 3. The service account needs a referent, not another proof burden

The account preserves Jim's testimony, but does not yet identify the service boundary in terms a new reader can picture: who encounters it, what they can do, which elements Jim originated and delivered, and what is now sustained by others.

**Question:** What concrete service experience should someone be able to describe after reading this account?

**Smallest resolution:** Use a compact contribution record separating service scope, Jim's actions, collaborative delivery, institutional ownership, present use and claimed consequences. Jim's account can establish his contribution; public objects can show the service and its capabilities. Neither needs to masquerade as the other. Adoption and measured benefit need their own evidence when claimed. This fills a communication gap without reopening truthful authorship as suspect by default. Priority: high; confidence: high that the operational referent is missing from this brief, not that it is missing from the underlying work.

### 4. The editorial baseline carries frames that may need contextual reconciliation

The LinkedIn work follows present corrections rather than importing website composition. The current [editorial guidance](https://github.com/jimCresswell/jimcresswell.net/blob/49b2addd53650ec0834629174b77a57cbb1d90b1/.agent/directives/editorial-guidance.md) still foregrounds SDK/MCP enablement, founder orientation and an implicit founder reading. The [CV source](https://github.com/jimCresswell/jimcresswell.net/blob/49b2addd53650ec0834629174b77a57cbb1d90b1/jcdotnet/content/cv.content.json) also describes the recent work chiefly as an SDK and MCP server.

These are not automatically false or contradictory. They may describe an earlier state, a different surface, or a narrower part of the same contribution. But an assistant told to align with them could reproduce the narrowing the brief corrects.

**Smallest resolution:** Record the few specific scope differences before composing LinkedIn copy. Preserve factual consistency and voice; do not impose identical emphasis or silently turn the LinkedIn task into a website rewrite. Asking Jim only when a choice changes the result provides the right boundary. Priority: high; confidence in the textual difference: high; its intended editorial resolution remains open.

### 5. Research completion and applicability are different claims

The desk research was reported complete while acknowledging only three partial comparative LinkedIn traces. That caveat is good. The residual gap is operational: which recommendations rest on direct evidence, general professional practice, platform facts or editorial judgement, and how well do they transfer to Jim's readers and aims?

The report and companion were unavailable here, so this review cannot evaluate their individual claims or accept their completion record as a fresh verification.

**Smallest resolution:** Reuse the research when available, checking only claims that carry a recommendation. Do not restart the general inquiry. Inventory can proceed without it; a recommendation labelled research-supported requires inspection of the relevant source. A completed-research status should mean the commissioned inquiry ended within its stated boundary, not that personal application or causal effectiveness is settled. Priority: high for research-based recommendations; source availability unresolved.

### 6. Full coverage needs a distinction between breadth and depth

Every element needs evaluation, and retention or deliberate absence can be appropriate. A rule to prioritise only after complete coverage could nevertheless be read as requiring equal investigation depth before any provisional triage.

**Smallest resolution:** Preserve a complete first pass over all sections and entries, then allocate deeper investigation by consequence. Permit provisional flags while making final priorities depend on the whole profile. Each relevant item still receives a disposition. Never infer absence from content that was unexpanded, unloaded or inaccessible.

Also distinguish an inventory whose access gaps are fully recorded from a substantively complete evaluation. Where an inaccessible item can count as addressed, its consequence must determine whether the overall assessment is complete or partial. Priority: medium; confidence: high in the possible completion ambiguity.

### 7. The access premise should be a checked state

An assumption of full authenticated read and write access is too broad. The original check established owner read access. Visible edit controls do not establish successful saves, access to every module or access to every audience view.

**Smallest resolution:** Replace the assumption with a short capability record: owner read, expanded content, edit controls, save verification, visitor perspectives. Verify writes only when implementation is authorised. Treat three source-access attempts as an upper bound where a new attempt could help, not a duty to repeat an unchanged failure. Priority: medium; read access resolved, remaining capabilities untested.

### 8. Inspection success does not establish reader success

Acceptance criteria that prove material was inspected, methods applied and recommendations prepared suit a desk audit. They do not show that a reader understands Jim more accurately or that opportunities improve, and the later learning plan remains generic.

**Smallest resolution:** Separate three states: audit coverage achieved; proposed composition judged suitable for review; reader effects still untested. For each major proposal, name the misunderstanding it is expected to reduce and an observation that would challenge that expectation. A later, separately authorised comprehension check could ask a relevant reader to explain Jim's contribution and a plausible reason to contact him. It would test intelligibility, not prove career impact. Priority: high; confidence: high.

### 9. A whole-presence scope and the observation sample have different boundaries

A whole LinkedIn presence is wider than the feasible method, which is a full profile inspection plus bounded activity and related-surface checks. A bounded sample of recent items is not a complete account of a professional network or of how recommendations circulate.

**Smallest resolution:** State the sampling rule, dates and formats actually inspected, with the reason for adding older items. Keep two observations separate: what is prominent now, and what selected older work demonstrates. Retain the unsampled remainder as unknown. Do not infer intent, engagement strategy or missed opportunities from silence or counts alone. Priority: medium; confidence: high.

### 10. OCE is both an instrument and part of the subject

Applying Jim's cognitive methods can improve the inquiry. Their use here does not independently demonstrate their effectiveness or Jim's professional distinction. Several OCE passes also share one analyst, one brief and overlapping sources.

**Smallest resolution:** Record the particular assumption each method exposed or the decision it changed. Judge public claims about the Practice against its actual creation, use and consequences. Preserve a plain account of the work as a comparator; if the framework vocabulary adds no reader value, method authorship can be evidenced elsewhere. This is a dependence limit, not an objection to using OCE. Priority: medium; confidence: high.

### 11. Private capture and stopping rules need small practical clarifications

Screenshots must exclude unrelated private data, and an authenticated owner page includes private prompts, analytics and surrounding account information. Capture therefore needs to distinguish profile content from account information.

**Smallest resolution:** Retain only necessary profile-relevant text and cropped captures; distinguish visitor-facing content from owner-only controls. Keep sensitive account information outside the public workspace. No broad browser export is necessary. Do not invent missing source contents.

The decision-based research stop rule also needs to preserve understanding as a legitimate outcome. End exploration when the consequential distinctions and remaining questions are clear; do not force every useful inquiry to culminate in a copy change. Priority: medium; confidence: high.
