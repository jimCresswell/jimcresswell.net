# LinkedIn content exploration — first round

28 September 2026 · Version 1.0 · Historical first exploration

This exploration records three alternatives considered before the [complete profile draft](../profile-draft.md). It preserves the professional analysis and unadopted wording from that first round; its references to a next conversation describe that earlier stage. See the [current editing notes](../editing-notes.md), [contribution analysis](contribution-analysis.md) and [dated profile baseline](current-profile-2026-09-27.md) for the working account. These alternatives are not approved LinkedIn copy or evidence of reader effects.

## Starting judgement

The most useful change is to make more of your contribution understandable in the profile itself. The recorded About says only “Web and cloud technologist”; the current Oak entry lists responsibility areas. Those give coordinates, but little sense of what you have brought into being, what decisions you have made, or how your contribution relates to other people’s work. This is a judgement about the content available, not an observation of confused readers. [Inventory: top card, About and E01](current-profile-2026-09-27.md)

Adding a comprehensive inventory would leave much of that problem intact. Someone could learn that you have worked in engineering, cosmology and research without understanding anything consequential about your work. Equally, an elegant story about origination could make delivery, teaching and sustained organisational work look like preliminary stages of the “real” contribution. They deserve to be understood on their own terms.

This first round therefore explores different selections and relationships. It does not choose a headline, promise an audience effect or supply an approved rewrite.

## What value would the content provide?

There are several plausible encounters, each with a different task. These are analytical scenarios, not researched personas.

| Encounter                                            | Useful understanding                                                                                     | A way the content could fail                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Someone who already knows you wants to introduce you | An accurate, portable account of your present work and a relevant example                                | They repeat a title or a grand abstraction because the profile gives them nothing more usable                      |
| A possible collaborator is exploring a problem       | Where your experience might help, how you approach consequential choices, and what would need discussing | A list of technologies invites the wrong conversation, or an all-purpose promise suggests expertise without limits |
| A peer arrives through an exchange or piece of work  | Enough context to connect that contribution to the person, without a full career lecture                 | About restarts a sales pitch instead of rewarding existing interest                                                |
| A leader or recruiter considers a role               | Recognisable responsibility, actual agency, collaborative scope and relevant evidence                    | Standard labels hide your range; inflated claims create incompatible expectations                                  |
| You consider whether to engage                       | A profile you recognise and can stand behind, which makes worthwhile differences discussable             | More attention is treated as success even if it brings unsuitable work or misrepresents your interests             |

The intended value is better mutual understanding. It can include recognising that a conversation or opportunity is unsuitable. Views, messages and flattering impressions would be weak substitutes. The research supports this as a reasoned design purpose, not a proven effect of particular wording. Its evidence base is weighted towards hiring, so it cannot settle all these encounters. [Research report, sections 1 and 7–8](../research/exceptional-cv-linkedin-research-report-2026-09-26.md)

## Three compositions worth exploring

These differ in what organises the material, not merely in tone. They are neither an exhaustive menu nor three rival identities. Elements can combine later, provided the result still has a clear job.

### 1. A clear introduction to the work you do now

**Organising question:** what does Jim contribute now, and what does that work make possible?

Begin with the recognisable current role, then explain a contribution in ordinary terms. The current service is a strong candidate: access to Oak’s curriculum resources inside AI assistants is more concrete than “OCE”, “MCP” or “agentic engineering” on their own. Organisational work needs enough presence to stop the account becoming a portrait of one technical project.

**Possible opening fragment — wording for discussion:**

> I’m Principal Engineer at Oak National Academy. I conceived and built Oak’s service that lets teachers find and explore its curriculum resources within AI assistants.

This makes a substantial action, its users and a capability visible. Oak’s ownership remains explicit. It is still a compressed account. It does not say who made every component, demonstrate that every assistant supports the service, or establish an educational benefit. The Experience entry and selected link would carry the next useful level of explanation, including curriculum specialists’ work, other technical contributions and extensive AI-agent assistance. That working arrangement is part of the contribution account; it need not occupy the opening sentence.

A subsequent movement could connect this to developing the Practice: the research, methods and tools for doing engineering with AI agents. That needs an intelligible example—such as maintaining continuity and checking work across agents—before another internal name earns space. It should not become an architecture tour. The innovation portfolio is not interchangeable with either the service or the Practice.

**Intended reader understanding (untested):** an interested person can describe a present contribution without needing the longer analysis. **What it loses:** judgement, relationships and the earlier career can become compressed background. Recent OCE documentation can quietly determine the whole profile. The service-led opening is a candidate, not evidence that this is the best first impression.

**Smallest version:** retain the current headline provisionally; replace the four-word About with a compact explanation; deepen the Oak entry. This could be enough. It is a serious alternative to a more elaborate narrative.

### 2. An account of consequential decisions

**Organising question:** what does Jim notice, choose and take responsibility for in real situations?

Start from a recognisable role, then use one decision that cannot be replaced by generic virtues. Oak’s rebuild is useful because it concerns delivery under constraints, with organisational consequences. It also counterbalances a possible impression that your contribution ends at imagining a new system.

**Possible passage — a contribution unit, not a complete About:**

> At Oak, I backed a platform rebuild as increasing complexity made change harder. I helped take the decision forward and manage the delivery risk.

This compresses the existing CV’s account. It preserves advocacy and responsibility without turning a collective decision into unilateral authority. A fuller Experience passage could say what was becoming difficult and what changed, using the extent of your actual contribution. It should not turn the platform’s subsequent life into a causal estimate of your personal impact.

The same composition could use HMPO’s whole-team specification workshops, or a choice in the curriculum service. It need not begin with the newest or grandest example. The purpose of context is to make the judgement intelligible; explaining a material constraint is useful even where older voice guidance cautions against justification.

**Intended reader understanding (untested):** a reader has something substantive to discuss—an actual choice and its consequences. **What it loses:** a single dramatic decision can eclipse sustained work, and several examples can become an interview anecdote collection. One instance supports a bounded account of judgement; it does not prove that you always recognise the right intervention.

**Smallest version:** one decision in About, one fuller account in Experience, and enough present orientation to keep the reader grounded. No “I have always…” conclusion is needed.

### 3. Work becoming possible with other people

**Organising question:** how does Jim’s contribution change what a team can understand, build or sustain?

The unit is the relationship between your action and collective work. This can make organisational contribution visible alongside technical creation. At Oak, the evidence supports structural design with colleagues, coaching and translation between technical and non-technical leadership. At HMPO, specification workshops connected people through examples. At HP, individual scripts became a collaborative tool. These are different mechanisms, not interchangeable evidence for “collaborative leadership”.

**Possible current-work passage — an alternative organising centre:**

> At Oak, I’ve helped shape the engineering organisation as it grew. I worked with colleagues on its structure, coached engineers and translated technical choices for non-technical leadership.

This draws on the same current CV account as the rebuild passage. It brings structural design, coaching and translation together around an organisation doing work. It still needs enough context in the full entry to show the scale and substance of those actions. It does not claim sole responsibility for growth or assign you the authority of everyone involved.

**Possible earlier-role passage — preserving some of the existing voice:**

> I got bored with manual testing at HP Labs and started automating it. The scripts grew into a team-built framework, then a sanctioned tool used at several HP Labs sites.

The current entry already says the work was boring and describes this progression. The fragment retains initiative, humour, collaborators and historical use while dropping the unverified present-tense “still used”. It may be better in the HP entry than in About. The word “boring” remains a tonal choice: it could give the account life, or suggest disdain for necessary work. Neither effect has been tested.

For Oak, the analogous account should preserve what you actually did. “Helped shape Engineering” needs concrete actions; “grew Engineering from four contractors…” on its own could suggest sole responsibility for collective growth. Team size can establish context without functioning as a personal performance metric.

**Intended reader understanding (untested):** technical work, delivery culture and people’s ability to act become visible together. **What it loses:** all work can be made to sound like facilitation. Your implementation, origination and direct technical judgement must remain present. “Creating conditions” becomes empty if the reader never sees what you created or changed.

**Smallest version:** one organisational paragraph in the Oak entry and one earlier concrete example. About could then remain short. This route does not require turning gardening, science or every earlier role into a metaphor for enabling others.

## What changes when we change altitude?

| View                                 | What becomes visible                                                          | Boundary to preserve                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| One sentence or card                 | Recognition, an action, an object                                             | Compression can remove credit and context; an appealing phrase is not a sufficient account  |
| One episode                          | Choices, constraints, collaborators, outputs                                  | One episode does not establish a universal working style                                    |
| A team or organisation               | Coaching, shared understanding, architecture and sustained delivery           | Collective achievement should not become either sole-person credit or anonymous “we”        |
| A service and its users              | Who can do what with the resulting work                                       | Available capability is distinct from adoption, reduced workload or educational improvement |
| A career over time                   | Range, continuities, changes and learning                                     | Later concepts must not be projected backwards as the intention of earlier work             |
| Public or institutional consequences | Why curriculum quality, professional responsibility and public benefit matter | A value commitment and a plausible mechanism do not demonstrate social impact               |

The people represented in the account matter as well as its readers. Oak’s curriculum specialists and engineering colleagues should remain recognisable contributors. Teachers’ professional authority should survive an AI-service description. Your agency should survive that attribution. These are composition and credit obligations, not grounds for making every paragraph defensive.

**Optional Obaith fragment, if you want its learning represented:**

> I started Obaith as a research project that I intended to turn into a startup. I later chose to stop. I learned a great deal, including the importance of delivery over perfect planning.

This closely follows your current account. Whether it belongs in public content is an editorial choice; factual support does not itself make disclosure useful. The record does not say excessive planning caused the stop or that the experience explains the whole later career.

[LinkedIn’s current Featured guidance](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a553419) says Featured content is not discoverable through search and requires a logged-in member to view. That is a reason to treat it as supporting evidence, not the only home of the profile’s meaning. [Recruiter filter guidance](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a414428) describes role, company, skill and keyword fields; it does not establish an optimal headline formula or a ranking benefit from keyword density.

## Where this leaves the next conversation

Keep all three compositions live for the next conversation. Each now has a current Oak fragment: a service capability, a consequential decision, and work on the engineering organisation. They expose different possible centres of explanation. No evidence yet warrants demoting the relational account to a supporting role. These examples also change which episode is selected, so their comparison cannot isolate an effect of framing alone.

The most consequential open issue is which conversations you want the profile to support, and what you want someone in those conversations to understand about you. That may change which example leads. It need not change the settled facts or turn the entire career into evidence for one objective.

A useful next step is to discuss these fragments and omissions with you, then develop a small set of About/Oak passages around what matters. One route may prove sufficient; there is no requirement to produce three whole profiles. Only after that would headline wording and supporting links have a concrete job to serve. If the compact version conveys enough, stop there. If a passage needs a contested fact, reopen that fact alone. Later review of selected copy in LinkedIn’s actual display would be a separate step; this round used the dated survey, not a fresh external or native-mobile encounter.
