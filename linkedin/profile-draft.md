## About

I’m Principal Engineer at Oak National Academy. I build public digital services and help shape the engineering teams and practices behind them.

At Oak, that has meant helping build the engineering organisation, backing a platform rebuild when growing complexity was making change harder, and creating new ways for teachers to use Oak’s curriculum. I conceived and built a service that brings its curriculum resources into AI assistants, where teachers can find and explore them while keeping their own professional judgement at the centre.

I also research and build the Practice: methods and tools for engineering with AI agents. It connects reasoning, collaboration and learning with the practical work of checking changes and carrying knowledge from one task to the next. Much of the implementation is developed with agents under human direction; deciding what to build and whether it is good enough remains our work.

Before Oak, I helped build the FT Web App, worked with teams at British Airways and the UK Passport Service on how they develop and test software, and researched cosmology. I’m interested in difficult questions, and in getting from an idea to something people can use.

Away from software, I keep an allotment and breed runner beans and sunflowers. My undergraduate thesis involved building a Theremin. Physics, growing things and making things still give me plenty to wonder about.

More about my work and interests: <https://www.jimcresswell.net/>

## Experience

### Principal Engineer — Oak National Academy

**January 2021–present**

I help set technical direction and build the engineering capability behind Oak’s public services. As part of a wider group, I helped shape Engineering as it grew from four contractors into five product squads and three platform groups: designing its structure, coaching engineers and translating technical choices for non-technical leadership.

When increasing complexity was making change harder, I backed a platform rebuild and helped manage the delivery risk as that decision was taken forward. The work combined architectural direction with the practical responsibility of keeping a live public service moving.

More recently, I conceived and built Oak’s service for finding and exploring curriculum resources within AI assistants. Oak’s curriculum is fully sequenced, fully resourced and pedagogically rigorous, released under the Open Government Licence. The engineering includes curriculum search, relationships between lessons and sequences, and reusable tools for developers building with Oak’s content. The teacher remains the expert: the service informs their judgement about what is right for their class.

Oak’s subject specialists create the curriculum. The service builds on their work and contributions from engineering colleagues; I led its end-to-end development, using AI agents extensively.

Alongside this, I research, design and implement the Practice: a framework and tools for engineering with AI agents. It brings together reasoning, collaboration, review, continuity and learning, so that knowledge gained during one piece of work can inform the next. This includes the methods agents follow and the software that supports their work.

## Publications

### Scale Dependent Galaxy Bias in the SDSS as a function of Luminosity and Colour

**Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society · 2009**

Galaxy populations cluster differently. We investigated how the relationship between galaxies and the underlying matter distribution changes with scale, luminosity and colour. The results show why these differences need to be modelled when extracting cosmological information from galaxy surveys.

Paper and full author list: <https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.1101>

### Cosmic microwave background multipole alignments in slab topologies

**Physical Review D · 2006**

We tested whether a slab-like cosmic topology could help explain unusual alignments in the microwave sky. In simulations, it made the observed alignment somewhat more likely, but even the smallest topology considered gave a probability below 1%.

Paper and full author list: <https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0512017>
